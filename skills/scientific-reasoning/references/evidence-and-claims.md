# Evidence and Claim Calibration

Evidence strength is claim-specific. A technically excellent dataset can still
be weak evidence for mechanism, and a small perturbation can be strong evidence
for direction but weak evidence for clinical generalization.

## Evidence Dimensions

Assess each claim on:

- measurement validity;
- design identifiability;
- biological replication;
- effect magnitude and uncertainty;
- robustness to reasonable analytical choices;
- specificity against alternative explanations;
- temporal ordering;
- perturbational support;
- orthogonal modality or assay support;
- external validity and independent replication.

## Claim-Specific Evidence Profile

Use four independent axes; on each report the actual evidence, limitations,
and the next discriminating observation. Do not assign an overall ordinal level.

| Axis | What to establish | What it does not substitute for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measurement | valid assay, specificity, scale, timing, direct versus proxy readout | design identification or clinical utility |
| Identification | temporal order, confounder control, intervention assignment, link-specific perturbation and alternatives | assay validity or transport to another population |
| Replication and robustness | independent biological units, uncertainty, prespecified sensitivity, independent dataset | causal identification from an otherwise confounded design |
| Transport and decision utility | intended-use population, external calibration, comparator, prospective decision performance | causality merely because validation is clinical |

An externally replicated clinical association can have stronger transport
evidence and weaker causal identification than a controlled model experiment.
Record both; neither is automatically a higher level. Temporal precedence or
dose response alone also permits confounding and is not a causal guarantee.

Do not average these dimensions into a decorative score. Identify the weakest
dimension that limits the requested claim.

## Claim-Evidence Table

For each important claim, record:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| claim | What exactly is being asserted? |
| scope | In which population, model, context, and time frame? |
| evidence | Which observations directly support it? |
| assumptions | What must be true for the inference? |
| alternatives | What else could explain the pattern? |
| falsifier | What result would weaken the claim? |
| evidence profile | Measurement, identification, replication, and transport evidence and limitations |
| next evidence | Smallest evidence that raises confidence materially |

## Common Overclaims

- Cell-level significance is not donor-level replication.
- Differential expression is not target engagement.
- Target expression is not drug sensitivity.
- A cell state is not a stable lineage or clone.
- Pseudotime is not elapsed time or ancestry.
- Ligand-receptor co-expression is not physical communication.
- A regulatory network edge is not direct regulation.
- Association after treatment is not necessarily treatment-induced.
- A model-system mechanism is not automatically a patient mechanism.
- Cohort association is not clinical utility.

## Wording

Prefer:

- “was observed in” for a descriptive pattern;
- “was associated with” or “differed between” for a replicated association;
- “preceded the response” for temporal order without identified causality;
- “contributed to the phenotype in this model” for a controlled intervention;
- “supports the proposed mechanism” for convergent link-specific validation;
- “was independently associated with clinical outcome” for external association.

State uncertainty and scope in the same sentence as the conclusion.
